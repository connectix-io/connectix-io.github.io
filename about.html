About

Who makes Connectix

Connectix is developed in Israel by people who build and run VoIP platforms for telecom companies.

Where it comes from

Our work before Connectix was the platforms telecom companies run: switches, SIP trunks, WebRTC softphones, call centers, billing. The telecom companies on the front page are ones we worked with on that. Connectix comes out of the same work: the question of what an agent could do on such a network if it had the switch's events and the call's audio, and a person to hand off to.

What we hold to

It works alongside the team.
Connectix takes the first pass on a call or a fault and hands the rest to a person, with a summary. It is not sold as a replacement for anyone.
It asks before it acts.
A lookup is free. A change on the switch, a transfer, a hang-up, waits for an approval, and the switch itself whitelists what the bot may ask for.
It runs on your servers.
One container next to your switch, or a desktop app on a PC. Events, call records and recordings stay on your hardware. The speech and language models are the providers you configure.
It writes things down.
The bot's instructions are files you can read and edit. Its actions, approvals and summaries are kept. A log line names the fact and the fix, never a credential.
It keeps running.
Elixir and OTP: supervised processes, bounded buffers, watched connections. A failure restarts one part, not the program.

What we do not claim

Connectix has no reference customers yet, so this site has no testimonials, no customer counts and no prices. The logos on the front page are telecom companies we have worked with on earlier platform work, not Connectix installations. The first pilot will be measured against the numbers that company already keeps, and we will write about it when there is something to write.

The code

Connectix is one Elixir application, built and tested in Docker, with an end-to-end test that drives the softphone against a test PBX. The Windows installer is built on GitHub from a tagged commit and published on GitHub Releases with a checksum file.
