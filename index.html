Connectix

Communication tool for the agentic workspace

We are dedicated to better communication.

Connectix is a phone, built on Elixir and the BEAM. Business calls, bot conversations and operational information in one application, with bots that stop and ask a person before a gated action.

A phone with bots inside

Connectix is one Elixir application on the BEAM, the runtime Ericsson built for telephone switches. Its bots run on Sagents: you define one, and Connectix gives it telecom tools, a voice path, memory and rules, on your own hardware.

Bots are versioned

A bot is a draft until you publish it, and publishing runs a preflight check. A published version is frozen, two versions can be compared as a diff, and each conversation stays on the version it started with.

Skills are code

A bot picks its skills from a catalog: event store queries, call reports, customer lookup in Fireberry, hand-off to a person, memory files, a task list, web lookup. Each tool declares its risk and whether it needs approval.

Rules are not prompts

Business hours, session time-outs, which tools stop for approval: these are checks in code, evaluated before the tool runs. The bot cannot talk its way past them.

A person in the loop

The bot does the reading. A person approves the gated actions.

Approve

Actions wait for a yes

When the bot wants to run a gated tool, the conversation stops and the request shows as a card: approve, reject, or approve always. Several requests come in one batch, in the bot's order.

Answer

The bot asks back

The bot can ask the person a question with choices, and carries on with the answer.

Take over

A person can take the conversation

One button hands the conversation to a person; the bot takes no further turns until it is given back. A reply already being written is not cancelled.

Hand off

The bot passes it on

A bot can hand a conversation to a person: it posts a summary of what was said and found, and stands down.

Jobs for a telecom team

Tier-1 support

A subscriber calls. The bot answers through mod_earshot and looks up the account by its business line in Fireberry: the line, the PBX domain, the trunks. What it cannot answer goes to a person with a summary.

NOC questions

The switch's events are stored: registrations, calls, hang-up causes, agents. Ask the bot what happened and it answers from the event store with reports, or free SQL when the operator turns it on. A switch that goes silent raises an alarm.

Lead and supervisor

Calls, agents and queues, talk time, hourly and daily breakdowns, with charts in the chat. The report queries are fixed and written by us; the model fills in the parameters, not the SQL.

The phone app

One page for the person beside the bot: a softphone and the bot's chat. It runs in the Windows desktop app, in the Connectix Client for Windows connected to a Connectix server, or in a browser.

Phone

Registers to your PBX over SIP. Calls in and out, hold, a second line with swap, call waiting, DTMF, blind transfer, recent calls with redial. When the line is down, it says why and what to check.

Chat

Talk to the bots, watch the reply stream in, approve or reject their actions, answer their questions, take a conversation over. Conversations, tasks and the bot's files in a side rail. The same bots answer WhatsApp Business, and a person can take a thread over and reply.

Logs

The SIP trace and the system log on the same screen, so a refused registration or a failed call can be read where it happened.

On your network

Events

FreeSWITCH Event Socket

Read directly, or through a small node agent beside each switch. One pipeline with bounded buffers, stored in DuckDB with retention.

Voice

mod_earshot

An answered call's audio comes to the bot over a WebSocket and its answer goes back. The caller can interrupt, and the transcript is kept with the conversation. Transfer and hang-up from the bot are planned.

Line

Its own SIP account

Connectix registers like any extension: the phone places and takes calls on it, and an API call can have a bot place an outbound call.

Start on one PC with the Windows desktop app. Later, move the same Connectix to a server next to your switch, where operators use the Connectix Client for Windows or a browser. On a server it runs today as one container (Docker, deployable with Kamal); a single self-contained Linux binary that runs as a background service, with no Docker, is planned. The databases (call records, events, conversations) stay on your hardware. Speech goes to Deepgram (speech to text) and Cartesia (text to speech), and conversation text goes to the language-model provider you configure: the embedded model gateway or an external one.

Built on Elixir and Erlang/OTP

A phone has to keep working while one call goes wrong. That is what Erlang/OTP was made for. The desktop app is one self-contained program: a single Elixir release with its runtime inside.

The switch runtime

The BEAM is the runtime Ericsson built for telephone switches. Connectix runs on it.

A process per call

Every call, every bot conversation and every connection to the switch is its own lightweight, supervised process. When one fails, that part restarts, not the program.

Side by side

Calls and conversations run side by side without blocking each other: the scheduler is preemptive and each process has its own memory.

Bounded buffers

Under overload a queue drops the oldest event, counts the drop and logs once, instead of running out of memory.

Watched connections

Keepalives, a deadman on a feed that goes silent, and reconnects with backoff from 1 s to 30 s.

A live console

The tray's Console… attaches to the running Connectix and looks inside without stopping it, over Erlang distribution, on the same PC only.
