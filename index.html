Connectix

Communication tool for the agentic workspace

We are dedicated to better communication.

Connectix is a phone, built on Elixir and the BEAM. Calls, the bots' chat and the logs in one app, and bots that work on your switch, your calls and your customers' accounts and ask a person before they change anything.

A phone with bots inside

Connectix is one Elixir application on the BEAM, the runtime Ericsson built for telephone switches. Its bots run on Sagents: you define one, and Connectix gives it telecom tools, a phone line, memory and rules, on your own hardware.

Bots are versioned

A bot is a draft until you publish it. A published version is frozen, and each conversation stays on the version it started with, so you can change a bot without changing what a running call does.

Skills are code

A bot picks its skills from a catalog: event store queries, call reports, customer lookup in the CRM, hand-off to a person, memory files, a task list, web lookup. Each tool declares its risk and whether it needs approval.

Rules are not prompts

Business hours, session time-outs, which tools stop for approval: these are checks in code, evaluated before the tool runs. The bot cannot talk its way past them.

A person in the loop

The bot does the reading. A person decides on the changes.

Approve

Actions wait for a yes

When the bot wants to run a gated tool, the conversation stops and the request shows as a card: approve, reject, or approve always. Several requests come in one batch, in the bot's order.

Answer

The bot asks back

When a fact is missing, the bot asks a question with choices instead of guessing, and carries on with the answer.

Take over

A person can take the conversation

One button hands the conversation to a person; the bot stands down until it is given back.

Hand off

The bot passes it on

A bot can hand a customer to the support team with a ticket summary, and say when the team will be back if it is after hours.

Jobs for a telecom team

Tier-1 support

A subscriber calls. The bot answers through mod_earshot, finds the account by its line in the CRM, checks the switch, and fixes what it safely can. The rest goes to a person with a summary.

NOC watch

The bot reads the switch's events: registrations, failed calls, hang-up causes, agents. Ask it what happened in plain words and it answers from the event store, with a chart when one helps.

Lead and supervisor

How many calls today, who answered, who missed, which agent is which extension: fixed reports with bound parameters, so the numbers are the same every time you ask.

The phone app

One page for the person beside the bot: a softphone and the bot's chat. It runs in the Windows desktop app or in a browser.

Phone

Registers to your PBX over SIP. Calls in and out, hold, a second line with swap, call waiting, DTMF, blind transfer. When the line is down, it says why and what to check.

Chat

Talk to the bots, watch the reply stream in, approve or reject their actions, answer their questions, take a conversation over. Conversations, tasks and the bot's files in a side rail.

Logs

The SIP trace and the system log on the same screen, so a refused registration or a failed call can be read where it happened.

On your network

Events

FreeSWITCH Event Socket

Read directly, or through a small node agent beside each switch. One pipeline with bounded buffers, stored in DuckDB with retention.

Voice

mod_earshot

An answered call's audio comes to the bot over a WebSocket and its answer goes back. The caller can interrupt. Call control is whitelisted on the switch and approved in Connectix.

Line

Its own SIP account

Connectix registers like any extension, so a bot or a person can place and take calls on it.
